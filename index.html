Five migrations, five employers

The through-line. Each row expands.

Objective-C→Swift Numerator · 2021 ▶

The hard part was concurrency, not syntax. Legacy Objective-C carried threading assumptions the compiler never enforced; behaviour that had quietly worked for years surfaced as race conditions the moment it crossed the bridge.

Diagnosed, fixed, and then covered — TDD and a linter introduced to a standing project so the class of bug could not return silently.

MVC→VIPER + RxSwift BBVA · 2018 ▶

The architecture came down from the global organization. My job was teaching it: three developers spanning junior, mid and senior — one of whom had built the old MVC app, and one of whom was a React Native developer new to iOS.

I got that engineer's first tickets accepted without knowing React Native myself. Shipped under strict code-coverage requirements.

UIKit→SwiftUI + Combine Numerator · 2021 ▶

Nobody had it on the roadmap. I made the case to the Tech Manager that it was even possible mid-migration, then to peers who were new enough to Swift that they had not considered SwiftUI an option — once introduced, they were enthusiastic.

Led roughly ten months of incremental adoption with two senior iOS engineers, setting guidelines and reviewing code. Incremental, never a rewrite.

Intel→Apple Silicon DiGinn · 2022 ▶

Inherited a non-runnable, stale project. Made the business case for the migration to stakeholders before doing the work, rather than presenting it as a fait accompli.

Retired stale dependencies in favour of Swift-native features, cut project warnings by ~90%, and left behind a CI pipeline that shipped 20+ releases.

Hand-written→Agentic CHOP · 2025 ▶

Team productivity had halted under unchecked AI code generation: output drifted from conventions, and the review burden landed on senior engineers.

I encoded the architecture and review standards into a spec-driven workflow the agents read before writing code — moving architecture from something policed in review to something satisfied before the first line exists. How that works →
